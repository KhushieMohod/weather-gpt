Agromet Farming Bulletin: Rain and Heat Stress Guidance

For farmers and livestock keepers in weather-sensitive districts. Plan field operations using validated local observations, the latest forecast, and advice from the nearest agricultural extension office.

Rainfall planning: delay pesticide and foliar nutrient spraying when high precipitation is forecast or rain is likely soon after application. Rain can wash chemicals from leaves, reduce treatment effectiveness, and increase runoff into ponds and drains. Inspect field drains before heavy rain, remove blockages, and avoid operating tractors on saturated soils. Harvest mature produce early when prolonged rain threatens quality, while protecting stored grain from moisture.

Crop protection: stake vulnerable plants, check greenhouse and nursery covers, and move seed, fertilizer, and harvested produce to dry elevated storage. Do not enter flooded fields during lightning, strong winds, or fast-moving water. Record crop damage with photographs only when conditions are safe.

Livestock heat stress: provide clean drinking water, shade, and cross-ventilation. Move animals away from direct afternoon sun and avoid transport or strenuous handling during the hottest part of the day. Watch for rapid breathing, drooling, weakness, staggering, or collapse. Separate an affected animal, cool it gradually with water and airflow, and contact a veterinarian promptly.

Worker safety: schedule strenuous work for cooler morning or evening hours, take regular shaded breaks, use protective clothing, and maintain oral rehydration supplies. Stop work and seek shelter during lightning, severe wind, or flooding. Local extension officers should be consulted for crop- and district-specific actions.

This bulletin is a prototype reference. Confirm current rainfall, temperature, wind, and official agricultural advisories before taking action.
